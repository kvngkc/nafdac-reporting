## LinkedIn

**Fake and unsafe products are a data problem as much as an enforcement problem.**

Every week in Nigeria, someone buys a malaria drug that does nothing, bread that smells of chemicals, or a "NAFDAC-approved" cream with a number that belongs to a different product. Most never report it. The few who do send in one complaint at a time, and a single complaint is easy to miss.

So I built a simple platform to change that: **https://nafdac-reporting.vercel.app**

**How it works**
→ Anyone can report a fake, expired, toxic, illegal or unregistered product in about 3 minutes, from their phone
→ No app to download, no account, and reports are anonymous unless you choose to leave a contact
→ You can add photos of the label, NAFDAC number, batch and receipt, and you get a tracking code
→ Reports are grouped by product, NAFDAC number, batch and state
→ The dashboard flags hotspots: a spike in reports for one product, the same product reported across several states, or cases where people fell ill
→ Pooled, reviewed reports are compiled into a form NAFDAC can act on

**How it's different from what exists**
NAFDAC already has good tools. Greenbook lets you check if a product is registered, the MAS scratch code verifies medicines, and NAFDAC runs its own reporting channels. This platform doesn't replace any of them. It links to Greenbook and NAFDAC's toll-free line, and it adds three things:

1. **Pooling.** Individual complaints become patterns by product and location.
2. **Breadth.** It covers food, water, cosmetics, devices and agro-chemicals, not just medicines.
3. **Low friction.** It's a web page that works on any phone, with no sign-up.

This is an independent citizen project, not an official NAFDAC service. I'd love to work with NAFDAC, state health authorities, pharmacists, consumer groups and journalists to make sure the data reaches the people who can act on it.

If you've ever bought something you suspected was fake, try it, and please share it with your network.
